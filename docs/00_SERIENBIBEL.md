# NACHTGLAS — Series Bible

> **Was das Licht vergisst, behält das Glas.**
> *(What the light forgets, the glass keeps.)*

---

## 1. SERIES TITLE

**NACHTGLAS**

A single invented compound word — *Nacht* (night) + *Glas* (glass). In-world it is the
name of a specific batch of hand-blown glass produced by the Hollerbrunn glassworks in
1893: pale blue-green, faintly luminous under moonlight, and the only material in the
world that light does not fully pass through — it *keeps* a little of every light that
ever touched it.

**Logo / wordmark:** see `brand/nachtglas_logo.svg`. Eleven-stroke geometric sans, letters
cut as though from a cooled glass pane, with a single hairline fracture running from the
crossbar of the **A** up through the **H**. The counter of the **A** holds a small amber
flame. On motion the fracture *travels* — it is drawn on, left to right, and the flame
ignites on the last frame.

**Format:** Season 1 — 8 episodes. Pilot runtime **11:20**.
**Medium:** High-end cinematic 3D animation, stylised-realistic ("believable anatomy,
illustrated surfaces"), 2.39:1, 24 fps.

---

## 2. GENRE

Supernatural mystery / small-town adventure / coming-of-age thriller.
Tone reference in one line: *an autumn night, a bicycle, and a street where the lamps
are going out one by one behind you.*

## 3. AGE RATING

**12+.** No gore, no graphic injury, no body horror, no on-screen death. The danger is
atmospheric and existential: darkness, disappearance, being forgotten, being *unseen*.
Nobody is ever struck, cut, or bloodied. The antagonist never touches anyone.

---

## 4. SERIES PREMISE (short)

In the small German valley town of **Hollerbrunn**, 111 antique street lanterns have
burned every single night for a hundred and thirty years. Nobody remembers why. When the
town council begins swapping the old hand-blown glass for modern LED fittings, people
start going missing — and worse, nobody notices, because the moment someone is taken,
every memory of them goes out like a filament. Five teenagers are the only ones who
still remember, for one absurd reason: each of them carries a marble of old glassworks
scrap in their pocket. Now they have to work out what the lanterns were built to hold
in — before the last one goes dark.

---

## 5. SETTING — HOLLERBRUNN

A town of 6,842 in a narrow slate valley in the German Mittelgebirge. Steep wet streets,
half-timbering repaired with concrete, a railway halt that sees four trains a day, fog
that arrives at 19:00 like an employee. It rains in the way small towns rain: constantly
and without drama.

| Location | Description |
|---|---|
| **Der Laternenring** | 111 cast-iron lanterns, gas-era, electrified 1961, all glazed with original Nachtglas. Plotted on a map they describe a closed ring around the valley floor, with the Brandhang at its centre. |
| **Die Glashütte am Brandhang** | The glassworks. Burned in the "Lange Nacht" of 1893. Roof gone, three brick kiln-domes still standing, floor a field of fused glass that crunches and glitters. Fenced, signed, entered by everyone under sixteen. |
| **Marktplatz & Glaspavillon** | Cobbles, a discount supermarket, and an iron-and-glass bandstand nobody has played in since 1998. |
| **Uhlengasse** | Narrow lane on the north-east edge. Lanterns 41–47. This is where the ring is broken first. |
| **Der Spiegelweiher** | Moor pond north of town. Always fogged, mirror-flat, no birds. |
| **Krammers Torhaus** | The gatehouse of the old works. Oda Krammer's glass-restoration workshop: a thousand fragments in labelled trays, and a lit lamp in every window. |
| **Tankstelle Rundling** | Petrol station on the bypass. Fluorescent, humming, the only 24-hour light in the valley. Kosta works nights. |
| **Kiosk Sahin** | The Sahin family kiosk on the station forecourt. Warm, cluttered, smells of cardboard and coffee. |
| **Gesamtschule Hollerbrunn** | 1970s concrete, lime-green corridors, a heating system with opinions. |
| **Der Nebelgraben** | The ravine below the Brandhang, where the fog pools and doesn't leave. |

## 6. THE MYTHOLOGY

### Nachtglas
Glass poured in 1893 from sand dug out of the Nebelgraben. Pale blue-green, slightly
cloudy, never quite cold to the touch. Under moonlight it holds a faint internal glow.
Looking *through* a piece of Nachtglas shows you what light has kept: the seam-lines
running lantern to lantern, the shape of the Löscher, and the people who have been taken.

**Nachtglas-Murmeln.** For a century, children in Hollerbrunn have dug marbles of
glassworks scrap out of the Nebelgraben and traded them in the schoolyard like currency.
Almost every child in town has one. Almost every adult threw theirs away.
*This is the entire reason five teenagers can save a town and forty-one adults cannot.*

### DER LÖSCHER — the antagonist

**Design.** Two metres forty. Thin as a drawn rod. A long coat that reads as poured black
glass: wet-looking, taking highlights nowhere. He has no face — instead a smooth oval pane
of dark glass in which you see *your own reflection*, dimmer than you are, and roughly a
third of a second late. His hands are long and jointed like a glassblower's shears. In his
left hand he carries the **Leerlaterne**, an empty lantern with no flame, and when its
small door opens, light in the street visibly *bends* toward it, the way smoke bends.

**Movement.** He never runs. He never hurries. He does not walk toward you — he is simply
closer each time you blink. In shot: he must only be moved on cuts, on blinks, or behind
foreground objects. **Never animate his approach.** That rule is the whole character.

**What he does.** He does not kill and he does not touch. He takes the light *in* a person
— their warmth, their presence, the fact of them — and the body goes with it. What remains
is their reflection: they are still there, in every window and puddle and phone screen,
standing, unhurt, mouthing words no one hears. And everyone forgets they ever existed:
photographs blank out, desks get moved, names fall out of registers.

### THE RULES (fixed for the whole series)

1. He moves only through unlit ground. Any light is a wall.
2. Nachtglas light he cannot cross at all. Modern LED light only slows him — it holds him
   about as well as a fence holds weather.
3. A person carrying Nachtglas keeps their memories. They remember the taken.
4. The taken are not gone. They are held in reflection, and they can see out.
5. To bring someone back, return the light that was taken: relight the lantern they were
   taken beside, using **original** Nachtglas in the fitting.
6. The ring of 111 lanterns is not a fence. It is a lid.

### THE LARGER MYSTERY (Season 1 spine — writers' room only)

- **Ep 1** establishes that the ring is a lid and that the council is unscrewing it.
- The 1894 town charter forbids the lanterns from ever being dark together. Nobody living
  knows who wrote the clause or why the glassworks owner paid for all of it.
- The pilot's final beat: the original blueprint lists **112**. One lantern is unaccounted
  for. It is not on any modern map, not in any inventory, and finding it is the season.
- The Löscher is not the thing the town was afraid of. He is what the town *did* about it.

## 7. MAIN CHARACTERS

### JUNO WENDT — 15
The one who notices. Practical, stubborn, allergic to being managed. Keeps a multitool on
a carabiner and fixes things before asking permission. Leads by refusing to leave.

**Look:** 1.62 m, compact and quick. Dark brown hair, tight curls, shoved up in a tortoise-
shell claw clip with two curls always escaping at the temple. Freckles across the nose and
one shoulder. Heavy dark brows, direct gaze. **Wardrobe:** rust-orange corduroy jacket,
cuffs frayed to white; olive cargo trousers; grey T-shirt; white trainers greyed at the toe;
a red carabiner at the left belt-loop with a multitool and a brass house key.
**Accessory:** a Nachtglas marble in her right jacket pocket, worn smooth by her thumb.
**Voice:** warm alto, fast, dry. Ends sentences early. Laughs in one syllable.
**Wound:** her mother works for the utility company removing the lanterns. Juno is going to
have to tell her that her job is what is eating the town.

### TAREK SAHIN — 15
The one who knows. Runs a local-history podcast with an audience of eleven, nine of whom
are related to him. Catastrophises out loud, at speed, as a coping mechanism — and then does
the frightening thing anyway.

**Look:** 1.81 m and still surprised about it; elbows and knees. Black hair, high fade, a bit
too long on top. Round wire glasses that catch every practical light in frame (use them —
they are a lighting instrument). **Wardrobe:** mustard-yellow rain jacket, always unzipped;
navy hoodie; dark jeans a little short; scuffed brown boots; over-ear headphones permanently
around the neck. **Accessory:** a battered handheld field recorder on a wrist strap; marble
in the recorder's case pouch.
**Voice:** higher, quick, breathy. Talks through fear. Cracks when he shouts.
**Wound:** his little brother Pepe copies everything he does, including the dangerous parts.

### NELL VARGA — 16
The one who already knew. Moved to Hollerbrunn nine months ago to live with her grandmother
and has made a policy of not explaining herself. Draws constantly. Has been drawing the
Löscher for weeks and told nobody, because who would she tell.

**Look:** 1.70 m, long-limbed, stands with her weight on one hip. Dark hair, undercut, heavy
fringe with the last five centimetres bleached to pale sea-green. Charcoal permanently in the
creases of her right hand. Four thin silver rings. **Wardrobe:** oversized dark-green surplus
parka down to mid-thigh, hood always up outdoors; black jeans; laced boots; a black
sketchbook with a rubber band around it, wedged in the parka's inside pocket.
**Voice:** low, slow, flat. Deadpan. Then, occasionally, unguarded — and it lands like weather.
**Wound:** she thought she was ill. She was relieved to find out it was a monster.

### BASTIAN "BASTI" ROHDE — 14
The one who does it. Youngest, broadest, kindest, cannot lie to save his life. Swims for the
club at 05:40 four mornings a week, which is why he owns a good headlamp and knows the town
in the dark. Believes in rules right up until a rule is in the way of a person.

**Look:** 1.75 m, swimmer's shoulders, still a kid in the face. Mid-brown hair, buzzed at the
sides, flattened by a swim cap. Ears pink in the cold. **Wardrobe:** navy club hoodie, faded
crest of the *SC Hollerbrunn 08*, chlorine-bleached at the cuffs; track trousers with a white
side-stripe; slides worn with socks (a running gag); an orange headlamp pushed up on his
forehead. **Accessory:** a Nachtglas bead on his kit lanyard — his grandfather's.
**Voice:** bright, loud, earnest, unmodulated. Says exactly the wrong true thing.
**Wound:** the woman signing off on the lantern replacement is his mother.

### KONSTANTIN "KOSTA" MEIWALD — 17
The one who saw it first. Works the night counter at the petrol station on the bypass and has
opinions about people who slam the door. Says one sentence where the others say nine. Older
enough to leave, loyal enough not to.

**Look:** 1.86 m, heavy through the shoulders, moves slowly and lands exactly where he meant
to. Shaved head six weeks grown out. A thin pale scar through the outer end of the right
eyebrow (bicycle, age 9, unglamorous). **Wardrobe:** navy work jacket with a name patch worn
illegible, over a grey hoodie; workwear trousers with a folding rule pocket; steel-cap boots;
a dented steel thermos that lives in his moped's top box.
**Vehicle:** a 30-year-old moped, mirror taped on, headlight aimed slightly too high.
**Voice:** deep, quiet, unhurried. Uses full stops.
**Wound:** everyone in town already decided who he is. He has stopped arguing.

## 8. SUPPORTING CHARACTERS

- **ILVA WENDT**, 41 — Juno's mother. Line technician, Stadtwerke Hollerbrunn. High-vis over
  a fleece, hands like a mechanic's, laughs with her whole face when she has slept. She is
  personally, physically removing the lanterns. She has no idea.
- **KATRIN ROHDE**, 46 — Bürgermeisterin, Basti's mother. Grey blazer, reading glasses on a
  chain, genuinely trying to do a good thing: *Lichtoffensive Hollerbrunn 2030*, sixty per cent
  less energy, brighter streets, EU co-funding. She is not a villain. She is a budget.
- **ODA KRAMMER**, 79 — glass restorer, retired, at the Torhaus. Cardigan, apron, magnifying
  visor pushed up in white hair. Every window of her house has a lamp in it, and she replaces
  the bulbs before they fail. Hostile, precise, frightened, kind in the last thirty seconds.
- **PEPE SAHIN**, 9 — Tarek's brother. Red anorak, one glove, a marble collection in a tobacco
  tin, and a complete inability to stay in a house.
- **ELIF TOPRAK**, 15 — Juno's classmate. Loud laugh, terrible at maths, wanted to be a paramedic.
  Taken in Act 1. Remembered by five people on earth.
- **FRAU BERINGER**, 34 — form tutor. Tired, decent, will not be able to help.
- **HERR DIESING**, 58 — school caretaker. Knows which lanterns flicker and has said so at
  three council meetings.

## 9. VISUAL LANGUAGE

**Palette.** Cool base — slate blue, wet asphalt, moss, fog grey. Two warm accents only:
the **amber** of Nachtglas lantern light (2200 K, soft-edged, with a visible glow ring), and
the **sodium orange** of the petrol station. The new LED fittings are a hostile 5600 K white
that renders skin grey and makes the fog look like paper. The audience should feel the
downgrade before anyone says a word about it.

**Supernatural colour.** One colour, used sparingly: pale blue-green Nachtglas luminance
(#7FE3C8 at the core, falling to #1B4D4A). Never used for anything mundane.

**Light as architecture.** Every night exterior is composed as *islands of light with dark
between them*. Blocking is: can they get from this island to that island. The frame should
teach the rule before the dialogue does.

**Rendering.** Physically-based materials; wet asphalt with real reflection depth; volumetric
fog with visible light shafts; anamorphic-flavoured bokeh with slight oval falloff; shallow
depth of field on close-ups (T1.8 feel), deep stop on establishers. Subtle film grain,
strongest in shadow. Very slight chromatic fringing at the extreme edge only.

**Camera.** Long lenses for surveillance and dread (85–135 mm), 27–35 mm for the kids
together so the group reads as a unit. Slow tracking, controlled dollies, deliberate push-ins
on emotional beats, handheld reserved exclusively for the Uhlengasse sequences — when the
camera breathes, the audience knows where they are. Reveals are done by *camera move*, not by
cut. Average shot length across the pilot: 5.6 seconds. No cut is shorter than 12 frames.

**The Löscher on screen.** He is only ever moved on a cut, a blink, a headlight sweep, or
behind a foreground wipe. He is never seen to take a step. He is framed centred and small
in wide shots, and never in close-up in Episode 1 — we hold the close-up back for Episode 3.

**Continuity discipline.** Character model sheets, wardrobe and prop assignments are fixed for
the entire episode; see `docs/02_SHOTLIST_VISUAL_DIRECTION.md` §Continuity for the locked
list (marble positions, headlamp battery state, jacket zip states, Pepe's single glove).
