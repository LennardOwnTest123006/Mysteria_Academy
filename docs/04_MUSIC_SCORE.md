# NACHTGLAS — Original Score

**Composed for this series. No existing work is referenced, sampled or imitated.**

---

## 1. THE ENSEMBLE

The score is built from **glass, wood and breath** — nothing that sounds like a normal
orchestra, and nothing synthetic that sounds synthetic.

- **Glass harmonica / rubbed rims** — 34 tuned glasses, wet-finger technique. The lead voice.
- **Struck bottles** — a rack of 16 cut bottles played with felt beaters. The percussion.
- **Detuned upright piano** — an old Hollerbrunn schoolroom upright, deliberately 18 cents
  flat in the middle octave, felt strip under the hammers.
- **Solo cello** — the only conventionally "emotional" instrument, saved for the family scenes.
- **Low strings (6 players)** — used exclusively as a floor, never as a melody.
- **Breath choir** — four voices, unpitched, recorded very close. Used for the taken.
- **Sub-bass** — one note, D1, used four times in the episode.

**No brass. No drum kit. No synth pads. No choir singing words. Ever.**

---

## 2. HARMONIC LANGUAGE

Home key **D minor**, but the score refuses its own tonic: the main theme circles the fifth
and only lands on D **twice in the whole episode** — in Sc. 22 (Pepe returns) and on the
final hard cut. The kiosk fridge hums a flat B♭, one semitone off the score's B♮, so every
scene in the kiosk is fractionally sour.

Metre: the main theme is in **7/8** (3+2+2), which never quite lets the audience settle.
The safe cues are in 4/4. The audience learns the difference without being told.

---

## 3. THEMES

| Theme | Instrument | Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **„Nachtglas" (main)** | Glass harmonica over struck bottles, 7/8 | Five rising notes, the fifth one falling short | The town, the mystery, the five of them |
| **„Die Fünf"** | Marimba, then the same five notes voiced across five instruments | Fragment of the main theme | Only ever plays when all five are in frame |
| **„Juno"** | Solo marimba | Four rising notes, unresolved | Curiosity; plays under every discovery |
| **„Pepe"** | Music box, 12 notes | Nursery-simple, in 4/4 | Warmth. Weaponised in Act 3. |
| **DER LÖSCHER** | *(see below)* | — | Absence |

### The Löscher's theme is subtraction.

He does not have a motif. **He removes them.** When he is near, whatever cue is playing loses
one instrument every four bars — glasses, then bottles, then piano, then strings — until
what is left is a single sustained note and then room tone. The audience learns to dread the
music *thinning*. By Act 3 they are frightened by an instrument leaving.

## 5. MIX PHILOSOPHY

Music never competes with dialogue. Every cue is written with a **hole in the middle**:
nothing between 300 Hz and 3 kHz except when nobody is speaking. The glass harmonica lives
above the voice, the strings and sub live below it, and the space where a German teenager is
talking is left empty on purpose.

The loudest music moment in the episode is 4M3, the final 12 seconds. The quietest is the
three seconds of black in Sc. 21 — which has no music at all, and is the moment everyone
will remember.
