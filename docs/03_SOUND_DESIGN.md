# NACHTGLAS 1.01 — Sound Design

**Mix:** 5.1 (stereo fold-down delivered) · **Dialogue anchor:** −27 LUFS dialnorm ·
**Programme loudness:** −23 LUFS (EBU R128) · **True peak:** −2 dBTP

---

## 1. THE SIGNATURE: „DER EINSTURZ" (The Collapse)

The single most important sound in the series. It is not a sound — it is a **removal**.

When the Löscher is near, the mix's **reverb goes to zero**. All of it. Rain, footsteps,
engine, voices — everything becomes utterly dry, as though the street had been replaced by
a small padded room. The world loses its size.

**Implementation:**
- All reverb sends automated to −∞ over 1.2 s, with the pre-delay shortening first (so the
  space *closes in* rather than fading out).
- Simultaneous 6 dB cut at 60–140 Hz — the town's low rumble leaves.
- Air band (10 kHz+) rolled off 9 dB. The scene gets smaller *and* duller.
- One added element only: a **descending glass tone**, a wineglass rim recording pitched
  down 2 semitones over 4 seconds, at −34 dB, felt more than heard.

Full Collapse occurs 4 times: Sc. 2 (Kosta), Sc. 6 (Elif), Sc. 16 (the flare), Sc. 21
(the three seconds of black). Partial Collapse creeps in twice more.

**The reverse of it is the emotional payoff:** when lantern 47 lights in Sc. 21, the reverb
does not fade back in — it **slams** back, full valley, wet stone, distance, the sound of a
world that has room in it again. Audiences should exhale here without knowing why.

## 2. HOLLERBRUNN AMBIENCE BEDS

| Bed | Contents |
|---|---|
| **VALLEY NIGHT** | Distant B-road hiss (never traffic detail, only the hiss), a river two streets over, drip from gutters at irregular intervals, one dog four times per episode, wind in wet leaves. Crickets **only** before mid-October — the episode is 14–16 October, so: no crickets, only a single late cricket in Sc. 4, which is a joke for two people. |
| **VALLEY DAY** | Rooks. A moped somewhere. The school bell at 8:00 and 13:25. A supermarket door chime that plays four notes and never the fifth. |
| **INT. KIOSK** | Fridge compressor humming a flat B♭ (this pitch is deliberate: it is a semitone off the score's key, and it makes the kiosk faintly uncomfortable all episode). Cardboard. A rack of crisp packets breathing when the door opens. |
| **INT. SCHOOL** | Radiator tick (7 s cycle), lino squeak, 200 unseen children behind two closed doors, strip light at 100 Hz. |
| **EXT. TANKSTELLE** | Fluorescent 50 Hz buzz, pump-nozzle clunk, the forecourt speaker playing a station jingle at the edge of audibility. |
| **EXT. SPIEGELWEIHER** | Nothing. Deliberately nothing. No birds, no insects, no wind. A pond with the sound removed. Used once, in the title sequence. |

## 3. THE LANTERNS

The lanterns are a character and get their own sound family:

- **Warm state:** filament hum at 100 Hz with a slow 0.4 Hz amplitude wobble; a moth ticking
  against the glass, one lantern in five.
- **Ignition:** a soft thermal *tik–tik–tik* of expanding cast iron, then the hum fades up
  over 900 ms. Never an electrical "click on" — these are old and they *warm*.
- **Extinguishing (natural):** a descending hum and a single relay clack.
- **Extinguishing (the Löscher):** **no sound at all.** The hum simply is not there any more,
  cut on a frame boundary. Silence where a sound was is the tell.
- **The ring closing (Sc. 21):** eleven ignitions in 1.4 s, ascending in pitch by the interval
  of the main theme, terminating in a low **thump** — a real recording of a bank vault door,
  pitched down and stripped of its mechanism.

## 4. NACHTGLAS / MARBLE VISION

- Raising the marble: high-shelf +4 dB, a bowed-glass tone fading in at −30 dB, and every
  ambient element narrowing to the centre of the stereo field.
- The **seams** of light have a sound: a slow travelling harmonic, like a finger on a wet
  rim, panned along the direction of travel.
- **The taken speak silently.** We hear the *effort* — a breath-shaped absence, an intake with
  no voice on it. Never a muffled voice. Muffled means "behind something". This is not behind.

---

## 5. THE LÖSCHER

- **He has no footsteps.** Not quiet ones. None.
- The Leerlaterne door opening: a tiny brass hinge, recorded and reversed, so the sound
  arrives *before* its own attack.
- Light being drunk: a reversed room-tone swell layered with a gas flame played backwards.
- His only forward sound is the pressure change — a 22 Hz sub-bass swell, inaudible in a
  laptop mix, deliberate: on good speakers the audience is uneasy before they know why.
- **In Sc. 21, for one frame, when the amber light passes through him, we hear a single
  struck wineglass.** It is the only sound he ever makes, and it is beautiful, and that is
  the point of Episode 3.

## 6. SET-PIECE CUES

**Sc. 6 — Elif.** Her tinny leaked music (original cue „Halbe Stadt", cleared, our own) is
the only warm thing in frame. On the Collapse it does not stop — it goes **flat and dead**,
losing all its reverb and top end, playing on for 1.8 s from an earphone lying on wet stone.
Then it stops mid-bar. Eight seconds of rain.

**Sc. 16 — the corridor.** Layer, and then subtract, in this order: five phone torches
(a real UI click each, deliberately pathetic), Basti's headlamp (a plastic detent), the moped
main beam (relay thunk + engine load), the flare (a chemical *whump* and then a hiss that is
the loudest sound in the episode). When the flare is drunk, the hiss **bends downward in
pitch** rather than stopping — the flame is being pulled, not extinguished.

**Sc. 21 — three seconds of black.** Absolutely nothing but five people breathing, close-miked,
distinguishable: Basti's is fast and shallow, Tarek's is wet, Nell's held, Kosta's slow,
Juno's steady on purpose. **This is the audio thesis of the whole show:** five people, in the
dark, refusing to leave.

## 7. FOLEY LIST (selected)

Wet cobbles under trainers, bike tyres on leaf mulch, moped kickstand, crate lids and straw,
a wooden globe crate set down on stone, cast-iron lantern head unbolted, crocodile clips
biting a battery terminal, sketchbook rubber band, tobacco tin of marbles (the single best
prop in the show — record it 40 ways), a marble rolling and stopping against a kerb, a red
anorak, an earphone on stone, keys on a hall hook, a chain door-latch, a magnifying visor
being pushed up into hair, chrysanthemums against a shop window.
