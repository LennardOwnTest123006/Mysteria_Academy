## 1. EXT. HOLLERBRUNN — VALLEY RIM — NIGHT (23:38)
**[MUSIC: 1M1 „Hollerbrunn, 23:38" — glass-rim harmonics, in from black]**

Black. Then a sound before a picture: a low electrical hum, the note a filament makes.

FADE IN on an extreme wide aerial. A slate valley at night, ridgelines black against a
blue-grey sky. Fog lies in the streets like something poured. And through the fog, warm
amber points of light — dozens of them — curving away in both directions until they meet.
A ring. A necklace of small fires around a sleeping town.

Very slow descent, 35 mm, drifting down toward the rooftops. Wet slate. A church clock
face lit from below. A single upstairs window still on.

Somewhere down there, one amber point goes out.

The camera does not react. It keeps descending. Two seconds later, the next point along
goes out. Then the next.

## 2. EXT. BUNDESSTRASSE / BYPASS — NIGHT (23:41)
**[SOUND: two-stroke moped, wind, tyre hiss on wet tarmac]**

KOSTA rides home, hunched, thermos rattling in the top box. Headlight aimed slightly too
high, painting the fog ahead of him. He hums. Badly.

He slows for the turn into the Uhlengasse and puts a foot down.

**KOSTA'S POV** — the lane runs away downhill, six old lanterns, warm amber, fog between
them like gauze.

The furthest lantern goes out.

He blinks. Lifts his visor.

**KOSTA**
Ey. Was soll'n das jetzt.

The next lantern goes out. Then the next. Coming toward him, unhurried, one every two
seconds, the darkness eating up the lane the way a fuse burns.

Kosta doesn't move. Handlebars trembling very slightly with the idle.

Two lanterns left. One.

**[SOUND: THE COLLAPSE — all reverb in the mix drops to zero. Rain, engine and wind go
utterly dry, as if the world became a small padded room.]**

The last lantern dies. And at the far end of the black lane, standing in the middle of the
road, there is a **tall thin figure with a lantern that is not lit.**

Kosta's moped headlight browns out — the beam physically *bends*, curving off-axis toward
the figure like smoke pulled by a draught.

**KOSTA** *(barely voiced)*
Nee. Nee, nee, nee —

He fumbles his phone up. Films. On the phone screen: an empty street. Nothing there.
He lowers the phone. With his own eyes: still there. Closer now. It has not moved.

He blinks —

— and it is **twenty metres nearer**, and we did not see it travel.

Kosta stamps the moped into gear. The rear wheel spins on wet leaves, catches, and he's
gone, engine screaming, the little red tail light dwindling up the bypass.

Behind him, one by one, going the other way now, the six lanterns of the Uhlengasse come
back on. Warm. Ordinary. As if nothing.

Hold on the empty lane. Fog. A moth ticking against glass.

**[MUSIC: 1M1 out on a single struck glass note]**

# ACT ONE

## 3. EXT. MARKTPLATZ HOLLERBRUNN — MORNING
**[MUSIC: 1M2 „Sechzig Prozent weniger" — bright, dumb, municipal; marimba, brushed snare]**

Daylight. Rain just stopped, cobbles shining. A cherry-picker is parked by the bandstand,
outriggers down. A Stadtwerke van. Orange cones. Twelve people watching, which in
Hollerbrunn is a crowd.

Up in the basket: **ILVA WENDT**, 41, high-vis, harness, gloves. She unbolts the head of an
old cast-iron lantern and lifts out a hand-blown globe, pale blue-green, the size of a
melon. Holds it a moment. Sunlight goes through it and comes out the other side *slower* —
a soft green bloom on her sleeve.

Below, **BÜRGERMEISTERIN KATRIN ROHDE**, 46, grey blazer, reads from a card.

**KATRIN ROHDE**
...und mit der neunten Laterne ist der Marktplatz offiziell umgerüstet.
Sechzig Prozent weniger Energie. Doppelt so hell. Und, das sag ich Ihnen
als Mutter: doppelt so sicher.

Scattered applause. Someone's dog barks.

**HERR DIESING**, 58, caretaker, hands in pockets, says it to nobody:

**HERR DIESING**
Hundertelf. Es sind hundertelf. Steht so in der Satzung.

**KATRIN ROHDE** *(pleasant, not listening)*
Ganz genau, Herr Diesing.

Ilva lowers the old globe into a wooden crate packed with straw. Eight others already in
there. She thumbs the dust off one and doesn't know why she does it.

**WIDE:** the new fitting snaps on. Flat white LED. In broad daylight it makes no difference
at all — and that's the joke the episode will stop finding funny by minute nine.

## 6. EXT. UHLENGASSE — DUSK (18:52)
**[MUSIC: 1M3 „Der Heimweg" — solo cello, one note per bar, absolutely patient]**

Fog arriving on schedule. Elif walks home alone, earphones in, one strap of her rucksack,

Six lanterns. Five of them warm amber. The sixth — the far one — dark, with a fresh

She walks through the amber. Her shadow spins slowly around her feet, handed off from lamp
to lamp. Lovely. Ordinary.

She steps into the gap where the sixth should be.

**[SOUND: THE COLLAPSE. Reverb to zero. Her music goes flat and dead, like the headphones

at a light that won't come on.

**ELIF**
Hallo?

Behind her, the fifth lantern goes out.

The camera does not move. It is a locked-off wide, 50 mm, and we are twenty metres back,
and we can do nothing.

**ELIF** *(smaller)*
Ist da wer?

The fourth goes out.

In the shop window beside her — Blumen Hesse, closed, buckets of chrysanthemums — her

**ELIF** *(a breath, not a scream)*
Oh —

**CUT TO:** the shop window. **Her reflection is still standing there.**

**CUT TO:** the pavement. **She isn't.**

One earphone lies on the wet stone, still leaking tinny music. Then it stops.

# ACT THREE

## 13. INT. SAHIN APARTMENT — PEPE'S ROOM — NIGHT (21:14)

Small room, bunk bed, glow-stars on the ceiling. **PEPE**, 9, in pyjamas and the red anorak
over them, is at the window with his tobacco tin.

**PEPE'S POV** — down into the street. A lantern goes out.

He presses his nose to the glass, delighted. He takes a marble from the tin and holds it up
to his eye, because his brother did it and he copies everything.

Through the glass: the blue-green seams. And at the end of the street, small and centred,

Pepe does not scream. He is nine, and he has never once been told a true thing about danger
that he believed.

**PEPE** *(whisper, thrilled)*
Der Laternenmann.

He goes for his shoes.

## 16. EXT. UHLENGASSE — NIGHT — CONTINUOUS

They stop dead.

**THE SHOT (locked off, 40 mm, 9 seconds, no music — just five people breathing):**

The lane runs downhill. Six lanterns. **Five are already out.** Only the nearest one, above
their heads, is still burning, and its glow ring is visibly *shrinking*, being drawn away
down the street.

And forty metres down, in the middle of the black road, small in a red anorak, standing
completely still with a marble held up to his eye:


And past him, at the bottom of the lane, tall and thin and centred in frame, the **LÖSCHER**.

**TAREK**
PEPE!

**PEPE** *(not turning, not scared, absolutely fascinated)*
Tarek! Guck mal! Er hat 'ne Laterne, aber die brennt nicht!

Ten metres.

**[SOUND: the flare's hiss drops in pitch]**

PEPE!
